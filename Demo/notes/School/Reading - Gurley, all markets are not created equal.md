# Reading - Gurley, all markets are not created equal

Bill Gurley, Above the Crowd, 2012. Ten factors he uses to judge a digital marketplace. Written from the investor side, but it works as a checklist for why a campus marketplace is a hard one.

Going through the ones that matter for textbooks and dorm stuff:

- frequency: he says higher is obviously better and lists Uber and OpenTable as high frequency. Textbooks are the opposite. A student needs them twice a year, so the app sits unopened for months. Strongest point against campus marketplaces, face it head on
- high fragmentation is good, lots of small buyers and small sellers. A campus has nothing but small sellers, so this one's on our side
- payment flow -> if money moves through the platform you can take a cut. A swap with credits has none. Fine for a student project, bad for a business
- network effects, obviously
- new experience vs the status quo. What people do now is a GroupMe message and a Venmo request. Low bar, but annoyingly good enough

Things I want to pull into the paper:
- Low frequency means you can't rely on habit. Every term you have to win the user back.
- When the status quo is free and good enough, people will keep using it alongside you. That's the multi-homing argument in another form.

Rereading on Oct 10: the frequency point actually supports narrowing to one college. If use is rare, you need the few people who do show up to find a match nearby, right away.
