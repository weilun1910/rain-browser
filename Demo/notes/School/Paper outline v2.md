# Paper outline v2

Platforms and Markets final paper. 5,000 words, due Dec 5. Draft to Hartley Nov 14. Revised after the Oct 16 check-in and sent to her Nov 1.

**Thesis (v2):** Campus marketplaces stall at zero, not at scale. The hard part is getting one residential college liquid, and multi-homing on free channels like GroupMe and Facebook groups keeps them from ever tipping.

## 1. Intro
- Campus marketplaces everyone has seen: textbook exchanges, sublets, free-and-for-sale Facebook groups, GroupMe chats
- Most student-built ones die within a year. Why?

## 2. Stuck at zero
- Network effects, direct and cross-side
- Critical mass and the chicken-or-egg problem
- The atomic network on a campus is a residential college, not the campus
- Multi-homing: when both sides post everywhere, the free channel wins ties

## 3. What's different about a campus
- Density: everyone in walking distance, one email domain
- Turnover and seasonality matter less than I thought. They make a weak network weaker, but they aren't why it never got going.

## 4. Case: Swapshelf in Branford and Saybrook
- Pilot Nov 9 to Dec 4, two adjacent colleges
- Waitlist by college, listings seeded by hand, share of listings with a request in 72 hours
- Compare with Facebook at Harvard in 2004 and Tinder at USC

## 5. Theory check
- Two-sided markets: Rochet and Tirole 2003, price structure over price level
- Who should be subsidised? On a campus it's the lister, because buyers are cheap to find and sellers are not
