# Book notes - The Cold Start Problem

Andrew Chen, 2021. He was at Uber during the growth years and then a16z. Read it for the paper, but it's also basically the Swapshelf playbook.

## The five stages
1. The cold start problem: getting the first network to work at all
2. The tipping point: once one network works, the next ones get easier
3. Escape velocity: growth that keeps itself going
4. Hitting the ceiling: saturation, spam, fraud
5. The moat: network effects as defence against competitors

My paper lives entirely in stage 1.

## Ideas worth stealing
- Atomic network = the smallest network that is stable and can grow on its own. For Slack it's a single team. For a marketplace it's one city, or one neighbourhood. Win something tiny first.
- Every network has a hard side that does more work and is harder to get. For Uber it was drivers. For a textbook swap it's whoever has to photograph and list the book.
- He calls doing by hand what the product will later do automatically "flintstoning". Reddit's founders posting from fake accounts so the site didn't look empty.
- Campus launches come up a lot. Tinder going party by party at USC, Facebook going college by college.

## Where I disagree a bit
He treats the atomic network as something you discover. On a campus I think you can just pick it, because the residential colleges already draw the lines for you.
