# Reading - Rochet and Tirole 2003

"Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets", Journal of the European Economic Association, 2003. The paper Hartley keeps coming back to in seminar. Tirole got the Nobel in 2014, mostly for regulation work, but this paper is the one people cite for platforms.

The core idea:
- A market is two-sided when the platform can change the volume of transactions by charging one side more and the other side less, even if the total price stays the same.
- So the price structure matters, not just the price level.
- Credit cards are the running example. Merchants pay, cardholders often get paid back in rewards.

Who gets subsidised:
- The side whose participation creates more value for the other side, and whose demand is more price-sensitive.
- Applied to a campus: listers are scarce and buyers are easy to find, so the lister is the side to subsidise. Swap credits for listing are basically this.

Multi-homing:
- If one side uses several platforms, the platforms compete hard for the side that uses only one.
- I think Armstrong 2006 calls this a competitive bottleneck. Check before citing.
- On a campus, buyers check everything (GroupMe, Facebook groups, the bookstore). Sellers usually post once, wherever is easiest. So sellers are the single-homing side and the fight is over them.

For the paper: sections 1 to 3 are enough. The math in section 4 is more than I need.
