# Swapshelf - idea

Same thing every September and January. Someone pays $160 for the intro econ textbook, uses it for one term, sells it back to the bookstore for $15, and a first-year two entries down the same hall buys a new copy.

**Swapshelf:** swap textbooks and dorm gear (mini fridges, lamps, rugs, desk chairs) with people in your own residential college. No money at first. You earn a credit when you give something and spend one when you take something.

Why inside a college:
- handoff is a two-minute walk, not a meetup at the train station
- everyone already half-knows each other, so less flaking and less weirdness
- Yale email plus college gives you identity for free

What people do now:
- class GroupMe chats ("anyone have the ECON 117 book?")
- the free-and-for-sale Facebook group
- bookstore buyback, which pays almost nothing
- leaving stuff in the hallway in May with a FREE sign

Risks I can already see:
- chicken-and-egg. Nobody lists until there are people asking, nobody asks until there's stuff listed
- only busy twice a year
- GroupMe is free and everyone's already in it

Sam is in. He'll do design and half the iOS work. I do backend and the other half.

Name check: swapshelf.app is taken but the App Store name is free.

Next: talk to ten people before writing any code. Paul Graham's startup ideas essay says the best ideas are problems you have yourself. I have this one every term.
