# Coffee chat - Nadia Brooks (Errandly)

Senior PM on the supply team at Errandly. Yale '21, found her through the alumni directory. 30 minutes on Zoom, Oct 14. She said I could follow up.

## How Errandly launched
- They never launched a city. They launched a neighbourhood. Astoria first, then two more in Queens, then one in Brooklyn. "A city is a hundred tiny marketplaces pretending to be one."
- The chicken-or-egg problem was all on the supply side. Customers will try anything once. Runners won't stay if they sit around with no errands.
- So they paid it down. Runners got a guaranteed $22 an hour for the first six weeks in each new neighbourhood, errands or not. Expensive, but it meant a request always got picked up.
- The founders ran errands themselves for the first month. She called it the only honest way to learn what the job is.
- They kept the map small on purpose. If you were three blocks outside the zone the app told you no.

## The metric
- Fill rate: share of requests picked up within 30 minutes. That's their liquidity number.
- Signups and downloads were vanity. A neighbourhood with 2,000 signups and a 50% fill rate was worse than one with 400 and 90%.
- They only opened the next neighbourhood once fill rate held above 85% for three weeks.

## Multi-homing
- Most runners also drive for DoorDash or Instacart. They open whichever app pays more that hour.
- So Errandly competes on earnings per hour, not on loyalty. Batching errands so a runner does three in one trip was the thing that worked.
- Customers multi-home too. They'll use TaskRabbit for furniture assembly and Errandly for pharmacy runs. She thinks that's fine.

## For the PM intern role
- The team wants interns who can say which single metric they'd watch and why. She said most candidates list five.
- Summer project is likely around batching or zone expansion.
- Apply through the portal and email her after. She'll flag it to the recruiter.
