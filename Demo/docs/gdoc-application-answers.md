# Application answers bank

Reusable answers for application forms. Edit per company, never paste as is.

## Why product management? (150 words)
I like being the person who has to decide what to build when the answer isn't obvious. On Swapshelf, my co-founder and I spent a week arguing about how items should change hands. What settled it wasn't who argued better. It was going back to nine interviews and noticing that seven people complained about flaking before we asked. I want to do that for a living: listen closely, make a call, and check whether it worked.

## Tell us about a product you love and how you'd improve it (200 words)
GroupMe. Every residential college at Yale runs on it, and it's great at the one thing that matters most for a group chat: everyone is already in it. Where it breaks is anything that needs an answer. A textbook request in a 400-person chat is buried within twenty minutes. I'd add pinned asks that stay at the top until someone marks them resolved, with a reply count. I'd measure it by the share of asks answered within an hour.

## Describe something you built (150 words)
Swapshelf, an iOS app for swapping textbooks and dorm gear inside Yale's residential colleges. I built the backend on Supabase with row level security that limits listings to your own college, and half of the SwiftUI app. The interesting part wasn't the code. Seeding enough listings before anyone could request meant recruiting 40 anchor listers by hand.
