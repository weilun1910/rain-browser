# Paper proposal - campus marketplaces

Theo Park, Platforms and Markets. Submitted Sep 30.

## Question

Why do student-built marketplaces on college campuses so rarely survive their first year, when campuses look like an ideal setting for a two-sided market?

## Why it's interesting

A campus has most of what the theory says a new marketplace needs. The users are dense, they're verified, and their demand comes at predictable times. Facebook used exactly this to launch in 2004. Yet most textbook exchanges, sublet boards and swap sites built by students die quietly, while the same trades go on in group chats.

## Approach

- Review the theory on network effects, critical mass and two-sided pricing
- Look at historical campus launches, successful and not
- Use my own project, Swapshelf, as a small case. It's a swap app for textbooks and dorm gear that will pilot in Yale residential colleges this fall.

## Early hypothesis

Turnover and seasonality. Every year a quarter of the users graduate, and demand only spikes twice a semester, so a campus network never gets the time it needs to build up.

## Sources to start with

- Rochet and Tirole (2003)
- Gurley, All Markets Are Not Created Equal
- Andrew Chen, The Cold Start Problem
