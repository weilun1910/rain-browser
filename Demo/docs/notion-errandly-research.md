# Company research: Errandly

Read before the Oct 14 coffee chat with Nadia Brooks. Updated after.

## What they do
A two-sided marketplace for neighbourhood errands: pharmacy runs, returns, picking up dry cleaning, waiting in line. Customers post an errand, a nearby runner picks it up.

## Size and stage
- Series B, about 180 people, based in New York
- Live in parts of New York and Philadelphia, neighbourhood by neighbourhood
- Revenue is a service fee on each errand plus a cut of the runner payout

## Competitors
- TaskRabbit for bigger jobs like assembly and moving
- DoorDash and Instacart for anything that's really delivery
- Runners overlap heavily with all of them

## How they grow (from the coffee chat)
- Launch one dense neighbourhood at a time and keep the zone small
- Guarantee runner earnings for the first six weeks so every request gets picked up
- Liquidity metric is fill rate within 30 minutes. Next neighbourhood only after it holds above 85% for three weeks.
- Runners multi-home, so Errandly competes on earnings per hour. Batching errands into one trip helps most.

## Why I want this one
It's the paper and Swapshelf at city scale. The supply team works on exactly the cold start problem I'm writing about.

## PM intern role
- Supply team, likely batching or zone expansion
- They want people who can name one metric and defend it
