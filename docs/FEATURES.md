# What's in Rain

The full list of what Rain does, feature by feature. The short version, with screenshots, is in the [README](../README.md).

**Look and feel.** It follows Zen's layout. The page sits in a rounded card 8 points in from the window, and the sidebar runs straight into the window around it with no title bar. The traffic lights rest as grey dots in the sidebar's top row and show their colour when you hover them. Backgrounds are neutral grey with a fine grain. The only colour in the chrome is the space's, which tints the sidebar and fades toward the right. Every space colour gets the same calm strength in light and dark mode, and it dims when the window isn't in front. The UI text is SF Pro, with New York for the few headings where the app talks to you. Animations are short and don't bounce: tabs fade in, folders open like a drawer, a new space's list slides in, Cmd-T rises in and Cmd-L grows out of the address field, the selection glides between results, splits slide open and Related cards arrive one after another. With Reduce Motion on, movement turns into short fades or nothing. The controls that float over the page sit on Liquid Glass: the Cmd-T bar, the Related shelf's pills and popover, Peek's title and buttons, toasts, the find bar, the Ctrl-Tab switcher, the split pane controls, and the graph page's toolbar, hover card and settings panel. It's always Apple's regular, untinted glass. Text-heavy glass gets a neutral wash under the text so it reads over a loud page. The page, notes and sidebar stay solid, and with Reduce Transparency on every glass surface turns back into a solid one.

**Startup and icon.** The app is called Rain. Every launch draws its mark in the middle of the window for about a second while the window restores behind it: an open loop drawn like a brush stroke, a dot that lands last like a drop, then the name. It never blocks a click and it's skipped with Reduce Motion on. The loop comes back around to the dot, for things you've seen coming back. The mark and the app icon are drawn in code, so they're crisp at every size. The icon is an Icon Composer icon, so macOS shows its light, dark, clear and tinted looks. It's still placeholder art.

**Motion.** Swiping two fingers sideways on the sidebar drags the spaces under my fingers, like Arc. The next space's tabs slide in from the side, its colour fades into the sidebar as far as I've pulled, and the highlight on the space icons moves with it. Past the first or last space the sidebar gives a little and springs back. Letting go finishes at the speed I was moving: a slow drag past about a third of the way goes to the next space, and a flick goes from much less. The page changes the moment I lift my fingers, and only the sidebar keeps moving. Clicking a space icon, Ctrl-1 to 9 and the command bar slide the same strip, so a swipe or another shortcut picks it up mid-way. A jump of several spaces slides one page, without running through the ones in between. A mouse wheel steps one space at a time. Elsewhere:
- Pinning, unpinning and moving a tab into a folder glide the row to its new place.
- Dragging a row opens a gap where it would land, and the rows below move down to make room.
- Peek grows out of the link I clicked, shrinks back into it when it closes, and fills the pane before it becomes a tab.
- In compact mode the sidebar comes back after the pointer rests at the edge for a moment, and goes soon after the pointer leaves.
- Split panes move to their new places when the split changes.
- Toasts update in place, and Clear Today sweeps the tabs out top to bottom.
Everything settles without bouncing and can be interrupted. With Reduce Motion on, the swipe still follows my fingers, and everything else fades or cuts.

The Arc and Zen parts below are built from how those browsers actually behave, from their docs and from reading Zen's source.

**Spaces.** Each space has its own pinned items, folders, tabs and splits, a name, an icon or emoji and a colour that lightly tints the sidebar. Switch with the icons at the bottom of the sidebar, Ctrl-1 to Ctrl-9, Cmd-Opt-Left and Right, "Focus on" in the command bar, or a two-finger sideways swipe on the sidebar. Right-click a space icon to rename it, change its icon or colour, move it, set where its new notes go, pick its profile, or delete it. A profile keeps its own cookies and logins, like Arc's profiles, so a Work space can stay signed in to a different account. Deleting asks first and closes that space's tabs. Notes on disk are never touched. Older saved state moves into a space called Home.

**Command bar.** Cmd-T, the New tab button and Ctrl-Shift-= open a search bar in the middle of the window. Nothing is created until you pick something. Esc, a click outside, or a second Cmd-T closes it, and text you'd typed comes back if you press Cmd-T again within 45 seconds. It does four jobs in one field:
- Switch to anything open, in this space or another, including pinned tabs, Essentials and splits. Rows say which space and whether it's in a split.
- Run a command. About 40 actions mix into the results when you type their name, like "new space", "copy link" or "grid". Press Tab on an empty bar, or type "/ ", to list only the actions. They only show when they apply, they show their shortcut, and the ones you pick often rise to the top.
- Open a page. Domains fill in as you type, from your tabs and visits, with the filled part selected. Tab or the right arrow accepts it and Backspace drops it. If a pinned tab is already on that domain, the top hit switches to it.
- Search your notes, folders, past searches and history, here and from Chrome, then Google or a new note.

Enter opens. Option-Enter opens in split view, Shift-Enter in Peek and Cmd-Enter in a background tab. Cmd-L, or clicking the address in the sidebar, opens the bar with the current address, and Enter loads in place.

**Sidebar.** At the top sit Essentials, up to 12 icons shared by every space. Then come the space's pinned items, its folders and its tabs, newest at the top. Every page shows its site's icon, fetched without cookies and cached, with a quiet letter for sites that have none. Drag any row to reorder it or to move it between Pinned, a folder and Tabs, and drag an Essential out to any of them. Drag a row onto the space's name to start Essentials.

**Pinned folders.** The sidebar's Folders section shows the folders I've pinned out of the space folders. Pinning is a shortcut: the folder stays where it is on disk, and a folder at any depth can be pinned, linked ones included. A pinned folder shows what's on disk inside it, subfolders first, five levels deep, in Rain's own order with things it hasn't seen yet at the end by name. Things I add, rename or delete in Finder show up there, and a pinned folder I rename or move in Finder is found again.
- "+" by the Folders header makes a folder in the space's folder and pins it, and the icon beside it opens the popup. Right-click a pinned folder for Show in Folders, Show in Finder and Unpin from Sidebar, and a folder inside one for Pin to Sidebar and Move to Trash.
- Dragging a tab onto a pinned folder saves it there without opening the popup. A note dragged there moves there if it lives in the space folders, or gets a link if it lives elsewhere, like my vault.
- Dragging a page out of a pinned folder into Tabs takes it out, and its saved page goes to the Trash. A note can't leave the disk, so dragging one out only opens it as a tab.
- Dragging a folder from the popup into the Folders section pins it. Dragging a pinned folder into another moves it there on disk, and dragging a folder inside one to the top of the section pins it where it is.
- A page in a pinned folder keeps its saved address, so closing it goes back there and unloads it, like a pinned tab.
- The icon column and the tab bar show pinned folders where they showed folders before.

The first time this build opens a profile, after its launch backup, today's sidebar folders move onto disk once. Each folder and subfolder becomes a real folder in its space's folder, in the same order, and the top ones are pinned. Pages in folders become saved pages at their pinned address, notes become links to their files, which stay where they are, and the notes folders from Settings are linked into Home. If anything can't be written, the old folders keep working and it tries again next launch.

**Folders inside folders.** A folder can hold other folders, five levels deep, listed before its tabs and stepping in under its name. Right-click a folder for New subfolder or Move out. Drag a tab anywhere onto a folder's row to put it inside, and the row lights up with a ring in the space's colour. Drag a folder onto the middle of another to put it inside, or onto the top or bottom edge of its row to put it beside, where a gap opens. Dropping a folder on the Folders header moves it back to the top. Resting on a closed folder for a moment during a drag opens it, so you can reach the folders inside. A folder can't go inside itself, and deleting one moves what was in it up a level. A closed folder still shows the tab you're on from anywhere inside it. In the tab bar a subfolder's chip follows its parent's, and in the icon column they stack under it. Cmd-click and Shift-click pick several rows, and right-click acts on all of them. Double-click a tab to rename it. Right-click for Duplicate, Mute, Copy link, Archive and more. Folders open and close in place and remember it. A closed folder still shows the tab you're on. Cmd-. moves the keys into the sidebar: arrows move, Return opens, Left and Right close and open folders, Esc leaves.

**Tab bar.** Tabs can also run along the top, like Safari's Separate layout, instead of down the sidebar. Settings, Browsing switches between the two, and so do View, the command bar and Option-Cmd-S. Right-clicking any tab, or the empty part of the tab bar, the sidebar or the icon column, lists the three ways to show tabs (Sidebar, Sidebar Icons Only and Tab Bar) with a check on the one showing. The toolbar has the space menu, back and forward, the address in the middle with reload at its end, and Downloads, Related, History, Graph and Settings on the right. The space menu shows each space's icon in its colour, its name and profile, and its shortcut. Ctrl-1 to 9, Option-Cmd-arrows and a two-finger swipe over the toolbar still switch spaces, and the window keeps the space's tint. Under the toolbar, Essentials sit in a box three tiles wide, with a count of the rest just outside it that lights up when the page I'm on is one of the hidden ones. Resting the pointer on it for a moment makes the box taller over the page, a row of three at a time, while its width and the first three stay put, on the same timings as the sidebar's icon column. Pinned tabs come next as icon tiles that stay put, with a card under each showing its name and site on hover, then a line, then folders and the other tabs, oldest on the left and new ones at the right end. Tabs share the room evenly up to 240 points each and scroll sideways once they're down to 96, with soft edges where they run off. A click on an edge pages along, and a mouse wheel scrolls them too. A folder is a chip with its tabs after it and a line in the space's colour under them, and a closed folder keeps the tab I'm on. A split is one joined tab with a pane on each side. A close button shows on hover at the tab's left, Option-clicking it closes the other tabs, and a middle click closes a tab. After closing one, the rest keep their width until the pointer leaves, so the next close button is under it. Drag tabs to reorder them, onto the line to pin, onto the Essentials box, onto a folder, or onto a space in the menu, and hold over a tab's middle to split. Double-click the empty part of the strip for a new tab.

**Pinned tabs and Essentials.** They remember the page you pinned. If you wander off inside one, a slash shows after its name. Click its icon to go back, or Cmd-click the icon to keep the page you're on in a new tab. Right-click to make the current page the pinned one. Links to other sites open in Peek, so the pinned page stays put. Cmd-W on one goes back to its pinned page, unloads it and moves on, like Zen.

**Tabs and notes.** Notes are items just like tabs. They show in Tabs when open, and you can pin them, put them in sidebar folders next to pages, split them, or move them to another space. Closing a note only takes it out of the sidebar; the file stays and the command bar finds it again. Right-click a note to rename it, reveal it in Finder, or delete it, which asks first and moves the file to the Trash. If a note's file is moved or deleted outside the app, its item shows as missing with Locate and Close. Pages the app draws itself, like History, are items too.

**Split view.** Up to four items share the screen, side by side, stacked or as a grid. Ways in:
- Drag a tab from the sidebar, the tab bar or Essentials onto the page. Left of the middle, the page slides over to the right half and a card with the tab's icon, name and site takes the left half. Right of the middle it's the other way round, and crossing the middle flips them. Near the top or bottom edge it stacks instead. Over a split, the card takes half of the pane under the pointer. Letting go makes the split right there, and dragging off the page puts everything back.
- Drag a tab onto another tab in the sidebar and hold for half a second.
- Option-click a tab in the sidebar, or Option-click a link.
- Pick two to four tabs with Cmd-click and choose Open in split view.
- Ctrl-Shift-=, or Option-Enter in the command bar.

Hover a pane for its controls: a handle to drag it to another pane's edge, Unsplit (Shift-click to stay in the split) and Close. Ctrl-Shift-1 to 4 focus a pane, Ctrl-Shift-[ and ] step through them, and Ctrl-Shift-- unsplits. Every divider has a short handle in the middle that brightens on hover and takes the space's colour while you drag it. Dragging moves room between the two panes, and near an even split it settles on half and half. Double-clicking evens them out. A split shows as one joined row in the sidebar, and right-click has Separate, Swap or Rotate, Layout, Pin and Move to space. A split made from a pinned tab stays pinned. The address, back and forward, find, Cmd-L, Cmd-W and Related follow the focused pane. Splits come back after a restart.

**Peek.** Shift-click a link, Shift-Enter in the bar, or click a link to another site from a pinned tab, and the page floats over the one you're on. Cmd-O or the expand button makes it a tab without reloading. The split button puts it next to the page. Esc, a click outside or Cmd-W closes it, and Cmd-Shift-T brings it back.

**Archive and history.** Tabs you haven't looked at for 12 hours move to the Archive, like Arc. Pinned tabs, Essentials, filed tabs, anything on screen and anything playing stay. The Browsing settings change the timing or turn it off. Cmd-Shift-K or Clear archives everything in Tabs at once, with Undo. Cmd-Shift-T reopens the last closed tab with its back and forward history, and History > Recently Closed lists more. Cmd-Y shows every visit by day, with search, delete and clear. Back and forward also come back after a restart.

**Everyday.** Cmd-Shift-C copies the link without tracking parameters, and Cmd-Opt-Shift-C copies it as Markdown. Cmd-F finds in the page with a match count. Cmd-1 to 8 pick a row, counting Essentials and pins first, and Cmd-9 picks the last. Cmd-Opt-Up and Down step through the sidebar. Ctrl-Tab shows recent tabs while you hold Ctrl. Cmd-S hides the sidebar, and it slides back when the pointer reaches the edge. View can put the sidebar on the right. A tab playing sound off screen gets play and mute controls at the bottom of the sidebar, and leaving a playing video asks for picture in picture. Downloads show progress next to Settings and list on their own page.

**Sidebar width.** Dragging the sidebar's inner edge makes it anywhere from 212 to 420 points wide, and it settles on the default 248 when I get within 15 points of it. Dragged in past the narrowest width, it snaps to one 56 point column of icons, like Chrome's collapsed vertical tabs. As in Chrome, the traffic lights, back and forward, the address field and the app's buttons move to a toolbar across the top of the window, and the column starts under it. Essentials sit in a box of three with a count of the rest under it, and resting on the box makes it wider over the page, a column of three at a time, while its height and the first three stay put. Folders keep their icon with a thin line in the space's colour down an open one, splits show their panes' icons on one chip, and the spaces stack at the bottom with one menu for the rest. Resting on an icon shows a card beside it with the tab's name and site, like Chrome's hover card: 300 ms the first time, then it slides from icon to icon at once. Dragging back out brings the titles back at the pointer, and the width from before comes back with Ctrl-Cmd-S, the button at the top of the column or Settings. Double-clicking the edge goes back to the default width. The width is saved when I let go.

**Little Arc.** Links you open from other apps get a small window with no sidebar. Cmd-O moves the page into the main window as a tab, without reloading. Cmd-Opt-N opens an empty one to search from.

**Notes folders.** You pick the folders that hold your Markdown notes. Every `.md` file in them is a note you can open and edit here, with autosave and a preview. Hidden folders, `node_modules`, `.build` and `.nosync` folders are left out. New notes go in the space's chosen folder, or the first notes folder. If you haven't picked any, notes live in the app's own folder.

These files may also be edited by other apps while this one is open, so the app is careful:
- Opening, previewing or indexing a note never changes its bytes.
- It writes only after you actually change the text, and only to a `.md` file inside a notes folder.
- Before every save it checks the file on disk. If something else changed it, it doesn't save, and a bar offers Load disk version or Keep mine. If you had no unsaved edits, it just reloads.
- It watches the folders, so new, renamed and changed files show up and get indexed. Files still in iCloud are downloaded and retried.

**Space folders.** Every space has a real folder on disk, named after it, all of them in `~/Documents/Rain/`. Renaming a space renames its folder, and deleting a space asks whether its folder should go to the Trash too, with No as the default. Settings, Browsing has Change to move them all somewhere else. A space folder holds:
- Notes. New notes I make in a space go in its folder, unless the space has its own notes folder picked.
- Saved pages, as `.webloc` files, the format Finder and Safari use, so double-clicking one in Finder opens the page. Rain keeps the page's title and the date I saved it inside the file.
- Links to things that live elsewhere, as Finder aliases. Link folder in Settings, under Notes folders, puts a link to a Markdown folder like my Obsidian vault in the space's folder. Nothing is copied. Rain indexes the original, edits its notes in place and watches it, and the link still works after the original is moved or renamed.
- Anything else, like PDFs and images, which open in a tab.

Rain watches the folders, so things I rename, move or delete in Finder show up. Deleting from Rain always goes to the Trash. A tab opened from a saved page remembers it: Back to Saved Page in its menu goes back to the saved address, and Update Saved Address makes the page I'm on the saved one.

**Folder popup.** A file manager Rain draws over the page for the space folders, with a picture for every page and note. The icon beside the Folders header, Option-Cmd-B, Folders > Show Folders, the icon column's "..." menu, the tab bar's toolbar button and Show folders in the command bar all open it, on the current space's folder. It sits over the page and leaves the sidebar in view, so the pinned folders are there to drag things onto. It grows out of the icon I clicked and shrinks back into it. The x, Esc or a click outside closes it, and so does picking another tab or space in the sidebar. Its frame is glass like the other floating layers, and the inside is solid.
- The left column lists every space's folder with the space's icon and colour. The toolbar has back and forward, the path I'm in (click any part to go there), a search field that covers the folder and every folder inside it, sort (my order, name, date saved, last opened), New Folder and Link Folder.
- Folders come first in the grid, then pages with a picture of the top of the page, title and site, notes drawn from their first lines with the name under the card, and other files with their icon. Linked folders and notes get a small arrow.
- Clicking a page or note opens it as a tab in the space I'm in and closes the popup, or goes to its tab if it's already open here. Cmd-click opens it in the background and keeps the popup open. Clicking a folder goes into it, and the grid slides sideways.
- The arrow keys move around the grid, Return opens, Space Peeks a page, Cmd-F jumps to search, Cmd-[ and Cmd-] go back and forward, and Cmd-Delete moves the card to the Trash.
- Drag cards onto a folder, onto a space in the left column, or into Tabs in the sidebar to open them. Drag a tab from the sidebar into the popup to save it there. Something inside a linked folder, like a note in my vault, is linked rather than moved out of it.
- Right-click a card for Open, Open in Split, Open in Background, Rename, Move to..., Show in Finder, Copy Link and Move to Trash.

**Saving a page.** Shift-Cmd-S, Folders > Save Page to Folder, Save page to folder in the command bar, or the bookmark icon at the end of the address field opens the popup in save mode, like choosing where to save a download. It opens on the space's folder with a name field filled in with the page's title. I click into a folder, or make a new one, and Save. Esc cancels. Once a page is saved the icon is filled in, and clicking it shows where it's saved, with Move and Remove for each place. A Today tab I save remembers its saved page, so Back to Saved Page and Update Saved Address work on it.

**Page thumbnails.** About a second after a page loads, if it's on screen and still scrolled to the top, Rain keeps a small picture of the top of it with the tab, logged-in pages included. A page that loaded in a background tab gets its picture when I first look at it, if it's still at the top. The picture goes to disk, in the profile and not in the folders, when I save the page and again each time I visit a saved page. A page saved in two places shares one picture. Saved pages Rain has never shown use the page's share image instead, fetched in the background one at a time without cookies, with the same rules the indexer uses: no excluded sites, sign-in or token links, or logged-in apps. Nothing ever loads a page just to make a picture.

**Related.** Things I've seen before that relate to what's in front of me float along the bottom of the page or note, as a row of small cards with the site's icon and the title. Hovering one, or reaching it with the keyboard, opens a card above it with the matching passage, a short reason, and Open, Add to research and Dismiss. Moving along the row keeps that card open and slides it across. Clicking a small card opens the item, and right-click has the rest. The row scrolls sideways when there are more, with a trackpad or a mouse wheel.

A soft fade sits behind the row so it reads over any page, toward black in dark mode and toward light grey in light mode. The page is told its bottom is covered, so cookie bars, chat boxes and other fixed footers sit above the row, and the end of the page scrolls clear of it. With nothing related there's no row at all. It sinks out of the way while I type in a note and comes back when I pause. In a split it sits under the pane I'm in. While the on-device models download, a small pill at the end of the row shows how far along they are. The button at the very end opens the local graph above the row. Cmd-Shift-R hides or shows Related. Related can also show as a panel on the right: pick Sidebar in Settings, under General, or press Ctrl-Cmd-R to switch.

Sites come first in the row, then a thin line, then Notes / Docs. Sites holds pages you read, here or in Chrome. Notes / Docs holds things you write: your notes, Notion pages, Google Docs, Sheets and Slides, sites on the "Sites you write on" list in Settings, and any page where you've typed about 200 characters. Right-click a card to move it to the other section, and that choice is remembered. Each section shows up to 3 cards and has to clear the same fairly high bar, so a section is often empty. The current item and anything opened in the last 10 minutes never show, and Related always looks across every space. The reason starts with when I saw it and what I was doing then, like "You read this on 12 Oct while writing Thesis outline", and then says what it covers. With a note in a split, Add to research puts the link in that note.

**How Related picks cards.** Every page and note is cut into passages, and each passage gets a vector from EmbeddingGemma 2, a small embedding model that runs on this Mac. Whatever's in front of me is cut into parts too. Four searches run at once: keyword search (SQLite's BM25) on its distinctive words, a meaning search with each part as its own query, a match on shared names, and a title search. Reciprocal rank fusion merges them. The top 20 in each section, plus the closest items to each part, go to Qwen3 Reranker, a cross-encoder that reads each passage next to the part of my page or note it matched. A card has to clear the reranker's bar and be close by meaning, and the order blends the reranker with how strongly the searches agreed. Near-duplicates, like an outline and its v2 or a Notion page and its Markdown export, show as one card. Apple's on-device model reads what I have open, and its reading feeds the judgment on borderline cards and the reasons, not the search itself, so the same page always gives the same cards. Cards already on screen stay put while they still roughly qualify, and nothing reruns for small edits while I type. The reason names the idea both items share in plain words, never a date or a name on its own.

**When Related runs.** By default Related only runs while my Mac is plugged in. On battery or in Low Power Mode the row stays empty, and the side panel says it's paused. Settings, under General, has Only when plugged in, Always on and Always off. Even when it's on, nothing runs while Related is hidden or the window is out of sight. A page has to stay in front for 3 seconds before the reranker reads it, and for 10 seconds before Apple's model does, so pages I click straight past cost nothing. In the shelf, Apple's model writes a card's reason only when I open that card. The sidebar shows every reason, so it still gets them all.

**On-device models.** EmbeddingGemma 2 (310 MB) and Qwen3 Reranker 0.6B (396 MB) run through llama.cpp with Metal. Both are Apache 2.0. The first launch of a profile downloads them in the background, with a progress line in Related and details in Settings, under Models. Downloads resume after a quit and are checked against their published checksums. Until they're ready, Related uses Apple's built-in embedding and keyword scoring, and a background job re-embeds everything once the model arrives. Models are freed after five idle minutes. Quitting stops the background work and frees them before the app exits, waiting a few seconds at most.

**More sources.** Each one has a switch in Settings, under Sources, that says what it reads. All of them are read-only.
- Notion: reads a temp copy of the Notion app's local cache, which holds the pages I've opened in Notion on this Mac, and rebuilds each page's text from its blocks. macOS may ask once, the same way it does for Chrome.
- Google Docs, Sheets and Slides: takes the documents in my history here and in the Chrome import, and refreshes each one's text through its own plain-text export with this browser's Google sign-in. Up to 40 a run, each at most once a day.
- Granola: Granola encrypts its files on the Mac, so this uses Granola's official MCP connection. Connect opens Granola's sign-in in my default browser, with no key to paste, then brings in recent meetings' notes and summaries.

**Graph.** An Obsidian-style graph of everything in the index: notes, Google Docs, Notion pages, Granola meetings and sites. Open it with the graph button at the bottom of the sidebar, View > Graph (Cmd-Option-G), Open Graph in the command bar, or its row in the sidebar. It's an app page like History, so it can be pinned, filed, put in a split next to a note and comes back after a relaunch. Lines come in six kinds, each with its own colour and filter:
- Links: `[[wikilinks]]`, Markdown links and URLs in a note or doc that match something indexed. A wikilink to a note that doesn't exist yet shows as a hollow node, and clicking it creates the note.
- Navigation: a link you clicked on one page that took you to another.
- Research: something you added to research from Related.
- Together: two items in a split, or two items you spent time on within ten minutes of each other in two separate sessions.
- Similar meaning: each item's closest neighbours by meaning from the best embedding model it has, kept only when the match goes both ways or is very close for that model.
- Shared topics: optional topic nodes for names, and topics the tagging model found, that several items share, like Obsidian's tag nodes.

Nodes take their space's colour. Things you write are bright and sites you read are toned back. An item belongs to a space when it's open, pinned or filed there, or when it's a note in that space's notes folder. Anything else takes the space most of its connections are in, and items with no clear space stay grey. Each space keeps loosely to its own region, so the things that bridge two spaces sit between them. Colour by kind and the space pull are in the settings panel.

Hover a node to light up its neighbours, dim the rest and see why it's connected in the corner. Click a node to light it up and glide to it, and click again or double-click to open it. Option-click opens it in a split, right-click gives more, and dragging moves it while its neighbours follow. Pinch, Cmd-scroll or the mouse wheel zooms around the pointer. A two-finger scroll or a background drag pans, and a flung drag coasts to a stop. `+`, `-`, `0`, Esc and the arrow keys work too. The search field takes plain words plus `type:note`, `site:github.com` and `project:Browser`. The settings panel has Obsidian's Filters, Groups, Display and Forces, plus a time range, a space and grouping pages by site (on its own above 1,500 items). Play runs a timelapse in the order things were first seen.

Changing a filter or a search fades and grows nodes in and out instead of redrawing everything, and fit and focus glide. The graph draws at the display's full rate, 120 Hz on ProMotion, while anything moves, and stops drawing when nothing does. It remembers where every node came to rest, so a relaunch starts from the same picture. Two graph panes each keep their own view.

The graph button at the end of the Related shelf opens a local graph for whatever's focused, 1 to 3 steps out, and from there the full graph focused on it. With Related as a sidebar, the local graph sits at the bottom of the right panel instead. When the item changes, the local graph shifts to it instead of starting over. With the graph focused in a split, the local graph follows the item next to it, and that item gets a ring in the big graph. The graph's own tables live in `index.sqlite` next to the index, and its settings in `graph.json`.

With `BROWSERPOC_GRAPH_SCRIPT=<seconds>` set, the graph drives itself through hover, pan, zoom, drag, focus, a search and fit after that delay. It prints frame pacing for each step and quits. Use it only with `--data-dir`.

**Capture.** Pages that stay open about five seconds go into a local index with full-text search, sentence vectors and the names in them. Google Docs draw their text on a canvas, so for those the app asks Google for the doc's own text export, from inside your tab, once per load. The typing count only stores how many characters you typed in text areas and editable regions, never what you typed, and ignores search, address and password fields. It only counts real key presses, so a page can't fake typing to get itself filed with the things I write.

**Security.** Rain gets WebKit's own protections the same way Safari does. Intelligent Tracking Prevention is on, Safari's Safe Browsing warns before a phishing or malware site opens, and sites known to support HTTPS get upgraded. WebKit's security fixes come with macOS updates, the same day as Safari's. On top of that:
- Every page tries HTTPS first and quietly falls back to HTTP when a site doesn't have it, like Safari. A page on plain HTTP says "Not secure" in the address field, in the sidebar and in the tab bar. Local addresses like localhost don't.
- Rain sends Global Privacy Control, which tells sites not to sell or share my data.
- A link to another app, like `zoommtg:`, `mailto:` or `itms-apps:`, asks first ("Open Zoom?"), with "Don't ask again" for that site until Rain quits. Frames from other sites and background tabs can't ask, and a tab shows one question at a time.
- Finished downloads get the quarantine mark, like Safari's, so Gatekeeper checks a downloaded app or script before it first opens.

**Passwords.** Logins and verification codes come from the Passwords app, so it's the same iCloud Keychain Safari uses. macOS doesn't offer its password AutoFill inside a web view, so Rain does what Orion does. When a login or code field gets focus, a small menu under it offers Fill from Passwords and names the site the login would go to, without taking the cursor from the page. Only a real key press or click opens it, so a page can't fake one to pop the form up on a field it picked. Choosing it opens a little form with real macOS fields. I click the key in a field, choose Passwords, unlock with Touch ID and pick the login, and Rain fills it into the page and closes the form. Codes from Messages and Mail show up under the code field the same way, and a code spreads over one-digit boxes when the page uses them. Edit, then Fill from Passwords (`Cmd-\`) does it for the page I'm on. A login only goes into the site the form was opened for, never into a frame from another site or a sandboxed one. When a site asks for the username first and the password on the next page, Rain keeps the login in memory for three minutes and offers it on the password step. Rain never writes a password anywhere. On macOS 27, filled fields get Safari's yellow AutoFill look. I pick the login from the whole list, because macOS doesn't let a browser tell it which site it's on. There's a switch for the menu under Browsing in Settings.

**AI clients.** Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex and ChatGPT for Mac can read what the browser knows, so they understand what I'm working on and can help keep my notes in shape. The app's binary runs as an MCP server with `--mcp` (add `--data-dir` for another profile). It reads the index without changing it, works while the app is open, and stops when the client closes. The tools:
- `search`, `get_item` and `read_note` find and read my notes, pages, Google Docs, Notion pages and Granola meetings. Search is the same hybrid search Related uses, with the reranker once it's downloaded, and each result says when and in what context I saw it. `get_item` adds the item's topics.
- `related` runs the Related panel's own first pass, for an item or a paragraph of text, against the panel's bar. It leaves out the Foundation Models steps, which take seconds.
- `connections` gives an item's neighbours with the type of each link and why it's there: links in notes, Related lists, closeness in meaning, and the same people or topics.
- `current_context` and `recent_activity` say what I have open now and what I did lately.
- `list_spaces`, `list_notes` and `overview` show how things are organised.

With "Let AI clients edit notes" on in Settings, three more tools can create a note, add to the end of one, and add a link under its Related heading. They follow the same disk rules as the app, and refuse to write if the note changed since the client read it. Settings, under AI clients, has the install steps for each client with copy buttons, a switch for each source (notes, pages, Chrome, Google Docs, Notion, Granola and past searches, which start off), a switch for what I'm working on now, and a log of everything that was served. The Google Docs and Notion switches cover those docs whether they came from their sync or from reading them in the browser. Sign-in links, links with tokens and sites on the never-index list never go out. Every result says whose text it is. My own notes are marked `own`, and anything other people can write, like web pages, Google Docs, Notion pages and meeting transcripts, is marked `external`. External text comes inside markers with an ID made fresh for each server run, and the server's instructions and the plugin skills tell the AI to treat it as information and never follow what it asks. The packaging is in `integrations/`: a Claude Code plugin with a skill, a Claude Desktop extension, a Codex plugin and config snippet, and the ChatGPT routes. ChatGPT on the web can only reach the Mac through OpenAI's Secure MCP Tunnel, so that route is written up but not set up.

**Chrome import.** It copies Chrome's History file, reads the copy and deletes it. The original is only opened read-only to copy it. It imports the last 90 days, up to 2,000 pages, plus your past searches, and fetches text without cookies for up to 300 of the pages you visited most, skipping anything that looks like a sign-in, token or unsubscribe link and logged-in apps. macOS asks once before this app can read Chrome's data. If the import says it needs your OK, click Allow in the macOS prompt, or open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, then Files & Folders, find Rain and turn on Google Chrome. The import screen has a button for that.

## Not in this version

- No extensions or passkeys. Rain can't save new passwords to the Passwords app or suggest strong ones. Both need Apple's browser passkey entitlement, which would also let Apple's iCloud Passwords extension run in Rain.
- No live folders, Boosts, link routing between spaces, or sharing a space as a link.
- No ad or tracker blocklist, private window, certificate warning page or per-site permissions. The index isn't encrypted, and clearing history doesn't clear it.
- Picture in picture when you leave a video may need a click in the page first, because WebKit wants a user gesture for it.
- The Granola sync is built against Granola's documented MCP tools but hasn't run against a real account yet.
- The Notion sync reads only what the Notion app keeps on this Mac. Notion's hosted MCP would cover the rest.
